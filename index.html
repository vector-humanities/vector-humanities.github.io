01 · About

Archives, sources and research tools now sit inside the vector medium.

Large language models and diffusion systems hold text, image and sound as positions in high-dimensional vector spaces, and they generate new material by moving through those spaces. In that geometry difference becomes distance and meaning becomes location, and the geometry shapes what can be represented, retrieved and said. Humanities scholars encounter a new mediation of their archives, their students' writing and their own research tools through the vector medium.

Vector Humanities documents new vector projects drawing on the Vector Lab and related tools. Each one has a humanities object at its centre (a book to translate, a corpus to read, an archive to edit) and works on it with methods and theory developed in the wider vector theory programme.

Vector translation

The Problem of Contingency

Kuki Shūzō, 1935
vector translated by David M. Berry

A complete English translation of Kuki Shūzō's Gūzensei no mondai (Iwanami Shoten, 1935), his study of contingency in its categorical, hypothetical and disjunctive forms, which has not previously been published in English in full. It is made from the National Diet Library scans of the 1948 printing, which are in the public domain.

The translation was drafted by a swarm of AI agents working through the page images under a shared brief that fixed glossary, register and conventions. Each section exists as a literal crib and as readable prose, each checked against the other and against the images. The terms, corrections and apparatus are the translator's.

Status: preliminary translation complete, October 2026. Being checked against the Kuki Shūzō zenshū and Omodaka Hisayuki's French translation (1966).

Research bench

Melville Research Bench

Billy Budd and the short tales
Michael Jonik and David M. Berry

A bench for reading, annotating and comparing Herman Melville's Billy Budd and his short tales in their versions: the manuscript's states of revision, the editions made from it, and the magazine and book texts of the tales. A joint project of Michael Jonik and David M. Berry (University of Sussex).

Billy Budd is read from the Melville Electronic Library's TEI, loaded from MEL's own repository at a fixed commit, not copied. The bench is built on bench-kit, the modules shared by the critical code studies benches.

Critical code studies

Spacewar! (1962)

A critical code studies reading
David M. Berry and Mark C. Marino, with Anthony Hay, Sarah Ciston, Art Schwarz, Claire Carroll and Norbert Landsteiner

A critical code studies reading of Spacewar!, the space-combat game written for the DEC PDP-1 at MIT in 1961–62. The original Macro assembly source is read as a cultural text, and a runnable PDP-1 emulator lets the reading move between the listing and the running program.

The Research Bench assembles, runs, compares and annotates the surviving versions; there is no single canonical Spacewar!. The principal output is a monograph, a companion to Inventing ELIZA (MIT Press, 2026).

Status: project site and Research Bench live; monograph in progress.

Critical code studies

SHRDLU (1968–70)

A critical code studies reading
David M. Berry and Mark C. Marino

A critical code studies reading of SHRDLU, Terry Winograd's natural-language-understanding program, written in MacLisp and Micro-Planner at the MIT AI Lab in 1968–70. Its blocks-world dialogue became symbolic AI's most celebrated demonstration.

The SHRDLU Research Bench, built on bench-kit, reads the source across its surviving implementations, with the program's paratexts catalogued alongside. A companion to the team's readings of ELIZA and Spacewar!.

Status: project site and Research Bench live.

03 · Methods

Methods and Approaches

Vector Lab

Instruments for testing interpretive claims against the measured behaviour of embedding and language models: Manifold Atlas, LLMbench, Vectorscope, Manifoldscope, Theoryscope.

github.com/vector-lab-tools

Critical code studies

Close reading of code as cultural text, applied to the code, prompts and agent briefs that do the work in each project.

github.com/critical-code-studies

Bench Kit

Shared modules for research benches: versions and their genealogy, comparison, annotation shared through Hypothesis, paratexts, findings and export. The SHRDLU, Spacewar! and Melville benches are built on it.

critical-code-studies.github.io/bench-kit

Vector translation

Agents work through page images in parallel under a shared brief; each passage is drafted literally and as readable prose, and each version is checked against the other and against the source. The translator writes the brief, settles the terms and corrects.

04 · Conventions

How projects are kept

  • ProvenanceEach project records its source edition, the scans it works from, and their rights status.
  • Marked handsMachine drafting is identified as such. Decisions on terms, readings and corrections are logged with their dates.
  • Instrument and materialWhere a model is used to read or measure, the project tries to separate what belongs to the instrument from what belongs to the material.
  • Open where rights allowPublic-domain sources and project outputs are published here. Third-party scholarship consulted in the work stays out of public repositories.
