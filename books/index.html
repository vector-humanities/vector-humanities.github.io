Vector Humanities Editions & Contingencies

Two open series of books

Vector Humanities publishes two series of books. Vector Humanities Editions publishes translations, made by the vector translation method, of works not yet available in English or not available in full. Contingencies publishes new short works commissioned by the series, in the humanities and in or about the vector medium. The series editor, David M. Berry, of the University of Sussex, takes on each book case by case.

Every book is free to read and download under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence, and is published with its workings: the decisions log, the build report, and the records of the faults found and corrected between text and typesetting. A translation is also published with its literal version, so that the reader can see what the readable text was made from.

Translations

Translations made by the vector translation method. AI agents draft the text from page images twice, once as a literal version, sentence by sentence, and once as readable prose, under a translator who writes the brief, settles the terms and corrects. Each version is checked against the other and against the page images.

Every translation names its source edition, the scans it works from, and their rights status.

Kuki Shūzō

The Problem of Contingency

偶然性の問題 (Tokyo: Iwanami Shoten, 1935)

Translated by David M. Berry

Kuki's study of contingency divides it into three modes, categorical, hypothetical and disjunctive, and defines it at its centre as the encounter of two independent elements. Drawing on Aristotle, Leibniz, Kant, Hegel, Boutroux and Heidegger alongside the Book of Changes, Buddhist texts and Japanese poetry, the book moves from logic through time, wonder, art and fate to the metaphysical absolute, and ends with an ethics of encounter. The translation is made from the National Diet Library's scans of the 1948 printing and gives the whole book in English, with Kuki's figures, notes on the translation choices, a concordance with the Kuki Shūzō zenshū, an index of persons and works cited, and a glossary.

III

From Proposal to Publication

  1. Proposal. A book is proposed from what its working folder already holds: the source edition and its rights, the state of the text, the latest build report, an outline and a sample. The series editor accepts, defers or declines it.

  2. Text. The translation, or the author's final draft, passes its own checks and is signed off as text. An author or translator other than the series editor first signs a short agreement.

  3. Typesetting. Vector Book sets the book from the same source files. Faults pass between text and typesetting as numbered returns and checks, each answered and closed.

  4. Book gate. A clean build report, no open returns or checks, and the translator's or author's sign-off. The series then writes the imprint page.

  5. Identifiers. An ISBN for each format and a DOI for the version are written into the book, which is rebuilt and checked once more.

  6. Publication. The book and its workings are published, the DOI is registered, and the book is listed here. A correction is published as a new version with its own DOI.

IV

How the Books Are Kept

Open access
Every book is free to read and download under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence. Anyone may copy, share and adapt it, including commercially, with credit. A translation of a public-domain text leaves the original in the public domain; the licence covers the translation and its apparatus.
Material from elsewhere
Material that belongs to someone else, such as an image or a long quotation, is outside the licence unless it has been cleared, and is marked as such on the imprint page.
AI disclosure
Machine drafting is identified as such. A translation states on its imprint page that it is a vector translation, and its Translator's Note describes how AI agents were used and what the translator decided.
Workings
Decisions on terms, readings and corrections are logged with their dates and published with the book, together with its build report and its return and check files.
Versions
Books are published in numbered versions. Each version has its own DOI, and a further DOI resolves to the latest. Earlier versions remain available. A translation marked preliminary is complete but not yet checked against every source the translator intends to use.
Corrections
A reported fault is recorded in the book's return files with what was found, where, and what was changed, and the correction is published in a new version.
